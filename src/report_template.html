Evidence of an AI-Authorship / Rewrite-Depth Shortcut

When rewrite depth differed between the labels, models could learn how heavily an article was AI-edited rather than whether it was true or false.

Edit-depth gap between the two classes
As built, the fake class kept far more of the source wording than the real class — a shortcut unrelated to truthfulness. Rebuilding the fake class to match the real class's edit depth closes the gap.

Is the gap real? McNemar's test on paired predictions

Almost everything is significant, which is why the exceptions matter. With ~10,000 paired rows even a fraction of a percent clears p<0.05, so a significant result mostly confirms the gap is not noise. The comparisons that came back null are the informative ones — they say two systems genuinely cannot be told apart.

Not length. A word-counter scores on the training data at every blend level, because real fake news (378 words) and synthetic fake news (376) are the same length. The shape also reproduces on the in-domain corpus, where length alone scores 0.474.

RQ3 — Which model is most consistent?

Consistency has two dimensions. Both are measured as F1 spread, so lower is better on each.
Data-composition sensitivity ↓ — how much does performance change when the training-data composition changes? (F1 spread across the six training recipes.)
Run-to-run instability ↓ — how much does performance change when the same model is retrained with nothing altered but the random seed? (Widest F1 spread across three identical runs.)
